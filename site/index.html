Think Ahead

A free, open-source chess trainer: openings against real games, tactics, calculation, and drills from your own Lichess or Chess.com games. It all runs on your device; nothing is uploaded. By Suhaas Narayanan (CoachSuhaas on Chess.com, suhaasn on Lichess).

New here?

Train on your own games

Think Ahead turns your games into a report card and drills, with puzzles, openings and endgames around them. No account, and nothing is uploaded.

Analyze my games
How it works
  • Start with your own games. In Analyze my games, type your Lichess or Chess.com username: you get a report card and drills from your mistakes (and the ones your opponents got away with).
  • Come back to Today. A short daily session: a few of your drills, five puzzles near your rating, one endgame, one opening line.
  • Openings: pick an opening or paste your repertoire as a PGN. The opponent plays real moves from Lichess games at the level you set, from 1000 to Elite, then Stockfish takes over.
  • Players: type an opponent’s username to see what they play, train against their openings and print a pre-round sheet. Watch: your own video library, with your place, notes and bookmarks.
  • Tactics, Calculation, Endgames, Play and Guess the move (add your own games too) for everything else.
  • On every board: right-click or right-drag for circles and arrows (Shift red, Alt or Ctrl blue).
  • Dark mode: Settings → Theme switches to dark.
  • Private by design: no account, nothing uploaded. Your progress stays on this device (Progress → Export to move it).

The idea

Analyze my games

Fetches your recent rapid and classical games straight from the Lichess or Chess.com public API — on your device; nothing is uploaded anywhere. Lichess games reuse Lichess’s own server analysis where it exists; Chess.com has no stored evals, so the last 25 games are analyzed with Stockfish on your device instead (slower).

Your own PGN files — over-the-board games from a tournament database, ChessBase, Lichess study exports (a study holds 64 chapters, so add as many as you need) or anything else. They are read on this device and never uploaded. Results are ready at once; Stockfish then analyzes every game without evals on your device. You can stop and carry on later: finished games are kept.

Help

What each page is for, and how the numbers work. Tap a question to open it.

What is each page for?
  • Today: a short daily session: a few of your drills, five puzzles near your rating, one endgame and one opening line.
  • Openings: play an opening against real Lichess games at the level you choose. Rated moves your opening rating and keeps the book and your file’s notes hidden until the line ends; Training shows them as you play and leaves the rating alone.
  • Analyze my games: a report card from your Lichess or Chess.com games, and drills from your mistakes.
  • Drills: the positions from your own games where you went wrong (or your opponent did and you let it go): find the better move.
  • Tactics: Lichess puzzles near your rating, with a puzzle rating from your first tries.
  • Blind tactics: a puzzle shown a few moves early. The moves that lead to it are listed but not played on the board: see them in your head, then solve it. Trains how far ahead you can see.
  • Calculation: long, hard puzzles where you enter the whole line, your moves and the replies you expect, before anything is played. Graded on how far your line follows the solution.
  • Blunder check: your own blunders (saved from Analyze my games), asked in two steps: first what your opponent threatened, then your move. Builds the habit of checking before you move.
  • Guess the move: play through a master game (or your own) from the winner’s side and guess each move, for points.
  • Endgames: textbook endings against perfect defence (the tablebase): win them, or hold the draw.
  • Play: a game against Stockfish at a set strength, blindfold if you like.
  • Players: what any Lichess or Chess.com player plays, with a pre-round sheet to print.
  • Watch: your own video library, with your place, notes and bookmarks.
  • Progress: your ratings and totals, and Export / Import to move them to another device.
What is Win%?

Your chance of winning, worked out from Stockfish’s evaluation the way Lichess does it, and always from your side.

From the evaluation

0.0 (level)50%+1about 59%+2about 68%+3about 75%+5about 86%

How each of your moves is judged

  • Inaccuracy: it gives away 5 points of Win% or more.
  • Mistake: 10 points or more.
  • Blunder: 15 points or more.

A pawn matters most when the game is level: +1 to −1 costs 18 points, but +5 to +3 only 11. Below 15% no move can be a blunder, because there aren’t 15 points left to lose.

Where you see it: Analyze my games, Drills and Blunder check. Lichess explains it at lichess.org/page/accuracy.

How do the ratings work?
  • Tactics and Blind tactics: an Elo rating against each puzzle’s Lichess rating. Only your first try at the first move counts, and a hint (or a peek in Blind tactics) counts as a miss. It moves quickly for your first 30 puzzles (K = 32), then settles (K = 16).
  • Openings: every line starts at 1800. Each move you find is scored by how hard it was to find: a move masters choose half the time counts as a 2200 find, a rarer one more. The line is rated once, when it ends.
  • Calculation: your score is how far your line follows the solution, every move of both sides. A different first move counts if Stockfish finds it as good.
Where do drills come from?

From your own games, in three steps:

  1. Open Analyze my games, type your Lichess or Chess.com username and run the report.
  2. Near the top of the report, the Drills from these games card has a Save button: press it.
  3. Open Drills. The positions where you went wrong, and the ones where your opponent did and you let it go, are there.

Each save is a set; you can remove it later from the same card.

When do puzzles come back for review?

For drills, Blind tactics and Blunder check:

  • Wrong: it comes back the next day.
  • Right: after 3 days, then 7, then 21.

Today puts what is due first.

Where is my progress kept?
  • On this device only: there is no account and nothing is uploaded.
  • A copy: Progress → Export saves it to a file you can import elsewhere.
  • In the app: Settings → Back up now saves it to the Files app (and it backs up after each session). Copy the backup to iCloud Drive to keep it if you delete the app.
How does the Apple Watch sync?
  • On its own: the watch has its own puzzles and keeps its own progress, so it works without the iPhone nearby.
  • To the iPhone: each result you play on the watch is sent to the iPhone.
  • And back: the next time you open Think Ahead on the iPhone, it records them and sends your progress back. The watch then shows “Synced”.
  • When: Settings → the iPhone app card shows when that last happened.

About, privacy and licences

About Think Ahead

A free, open-source chess trainer: openings against real games, tactics, calculation, blind tactics, endgames, and drills from your own games. It runs entirely on your device: in your browser on the web, and here in the app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. There is no account and no server of ours: the site is a set of files.

Privacy

In effect since 18 November 2026. This is the privacy policy of Think Ahead: the website and the app for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch.

We collect nothing

There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking and no cookies of ours. We have no server that receives anything from you, so we never see your games, your progress or any other data.

Where your progress is

  • On the website: in this browser's own storage, on this device only. Progress → Export progress saves it as a file; clearing the browser's data deletes it.
  • In the app on iPhone or iPad: on that device, and copied after each session to a backup file in the Files app (On My iPhone or On My iPad → Think Ahead), which goes into the device's own backups. On the Apple Watch: on the watch. The watch and the iPhone exchange your results directly, through Apple's WatchConnectivity; nothing passes through us. Review reminders are scheduled on the iPhone or iPad itself. Deleting the app deletes its progress (and the backup in Files, unless you moved it).

What leaves your device, and only when you ask

  • Your games: when you type a username in Analyze my games or Players, your device asks the public Lichess API (lichess.org) or the Chess.com API (api.chess.com) for that player's games. A Lichess study link you paste is fetched from Lichess the same way.
  • Endgames: positions off the stored lines are sent to the Lichess tablebase.
  • Review on Lichess: sends that game's moves to Lichess to open its analysis board.
  • Watch: "Get title" asks YouTube (www.youtube.com) for a video's title.
  • The engine: Stockfish ships with the site and the app. On the website only, if your browser can't run that copy, one is fetched from unpkg.com or cdnjs.cloudflare.com.

Those services receive the request from your device (and so its IP address), under their own privacy policies. The website itself is served by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees each request for its files (GitHub's privacy statement). Links to Lichess, Chess.com or YouTube open those sites, under their own policies.

Children, changes, questions

Nothing is collected from anyone, children included. If this policy changes, this page changes, with a new date. Questions: open an issue on GitHub (issues are public, so please don't include anything private).
